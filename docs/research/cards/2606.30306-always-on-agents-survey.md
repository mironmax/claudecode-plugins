# Always-On Agents: A Survey of Persistent Memory, State, and Governance in LLM Agents

arXiv [2606.30306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30306) v1, 29 Jun 2026.

**Claim.** The agent-memory literature concentrates on accumulating and
recalling state rather than governing it. In a coded corpus of 435 works,
retrieve appears in 269 and write in 200, while rollback appears in 27.
Authority is the rarest of six axes at 72. "None in the corpus reports
recovery success or cost after corruption."

**Conditions.**
- Method: a coded, scoped literature map, "not a census". Each work is coded
  by lifecycle stage (write, validate, organize, retrieve, act, update,
  forget, audit, rollback) and by six axes: authority, scope, mutability,
  provenance, recoverability, actionability.
- Pilot protocol (AOEP-v0): nine fault patterns plus three episodes and three
  execution traces, scored with readers from 3B to 8B. By the authors'
  account, this is a configuration-level pilot, not a benchmark.
- No model is trained or deployed; this is a survey.

**Applicability: partially.** Several axes carry little weight for a
single-user personal store: authority and permission, multi-principal scope.
Two carry more:
- **Provenance**, which the survey defines as not losing the episodic source
  when an item is consolidated. A node's `touches` can point to session notes,
  and the storage root's git history holds prior versions; whether a given
  node's claim can be traced to its evidence depends on whether those touches
  were written.
- **Recoverability**. The survey's "controlled compounding" criterion is
  falsifiable: when a regression is traced to a specific write, can that write
  be identified, de-authorized and reverted, together with its derived state?

**What it would change here.** A concrete exercise rather than a feature. Take
one chore-made change from `chores.jsonl` and try to revert it using only git
history and the progress trail. Record what could not be undone: for example,
edges other nodes gained because of it, or archival decisions made while it
was in place. The result shows whether the plugin already meets the
criterion, and where it does not.

**Verification.** Partial read: abstract, §1.1 (corpus and its skew), §2.3
(the six axes), §4.2.2–4.2.3 (validate, organize), §6.4.2–6.6 (adaptation
turning net-negative, baseline-beats-memory, forgetting, controlled

body was not read. The corpus counts are the authors' coding, not
independently checked.
