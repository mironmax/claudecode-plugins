# Rethinking Continual Experience Internalization for Self-Evolving LLM Agents

arXiv [2606.04703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04703) v1, 3 Jun 2026. Renmin University of China.

**Claim.** When experience is distilled into model weights over several
iterations, instance-level experience gives transient gains that fall below
the base model, while principle-level experience holds. Step-wise injection
into the teacher beats global injection: WebWalkerQA 23.2% to 31.2% in one
iteration with self-generated experience. By the third iteration,
global-injection models answer without any tool call in 63.82% of cases,
against 0% for step-wise.

**Conditions.**
- Models: Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and Qwen3-8B students; experience
  summarised by DeepSeek-V4.
- Tasks: web-reasoning QA with five tools. Training draws on 15K examples;
  evaluation uses WebWalkerQA (in-domain), GAIA-Text-103 and BrowseComp-ZH.
- Memory: natural-language experience pools that become supervision for
  context distillation into weights (on-policy or off-policy).
- Who writes, and when: an LLM summarises each iteration's trajectories; the
  pool is rebuilt every iteration from the updated policy.
- Horizon: a few self-evolution iterations, each a full training run.
- Content profile of instance-level items: 74.4% contain URLs or domains,
  57.3% concrete numbers, 93.9% query- or entity-specific strings. 84.0% of
  principle-level items contain strategy-like statements, against 3.7% of
  instance-level ones.

**Applicability: does not hold directly.** The mechanism is weight updates on
small models. This plugin never trains, and its consumer is a frontier model
reading text. "Injection" here means what the *teacher* sees while
supervision is being built, not retrieval at inference time. Treating it as
evidence for tool-level recall would be an analogy, not a finding. The
granularity result is consistent with 2604.14004, but it was obtained in a
different regime.

**What it would change here.** Its content-profile measures (URLs, concrete
numbers, entity strings) are a cheap candidate signal for "instance-shaped"
alongside the date-in-id heuristic in task 03. Testable: on a hand-labelled
sample of nodes, measure how far each signal agrees with human labels. A
caveat applies: in a declarative store, a specific path or number is often
the point of a node, so this signal is expected to over-flag here.
